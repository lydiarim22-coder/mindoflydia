College application season can make every date feel urgent. There are essays to finish, recommendations to request, and enough abbreviations to fill a notebook. Then another one appears: ED2.

If your teenager is considering Early Decision II, I would put two conversations on the calendar now: which school they would truly choose, and what your family needs to understand before making that commitment.

January may sound far away in October. But a deadline on New Year's Day leaves very little room for a last-minute family discussion.

## What is Early Decision II?

Early Decision II, often called ED2 or ED II, is a later Early Decision application round offered by some colleges. It gives students more time to decide on a first-choice school and prepare their application.

The later timing does not make it a casual commitment. ED2 is binding: students agree to enroll if admitted under the terms of the school's Early Decision agreement. Early Action is different and generally does not require that commitment. [College Board's explanation of Early Decision and Early Action](https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org/plan-for-college/apply-to-college/facts-about-early-decision-and-early-action) is a useful place to understand the distinction.

A school can become a student's first choice later in the process. The important question is whether the student is ready to make that choice.

## Do not put every ED2 deadline on January 15

College Intel's [ED2 Deadlines by School: The Real January 2027 Dates](https://collegeintel.fuselyn.com/articles/ed2-deadlines-by-school) is a helpful starting point for building a calendar. The article identifies January 15 as the most common ED2 date among the schools it tracks, while showing that other schools close earlier or later.

That distinction matters. A broad reminder to "apply in January" can leave a family preparing for the wrong week.

## The application deadline is only one date

For each school, I would keep separate calendar entries for the application, financial aid forms, and any scholarship or program requirements.

Vanderbilt, for example, lists a January 1, 2027 ED2 application deadline and a January 1 priority filing deadline for the CSS Profile and FAFSA. Its merit scholarship application deadline is earlier, on December 1, 2026. Those dates appear together on [Vanderbilt's admissions page](https://admissions.vanderbilt.edu/apply/), but they serve different purposes.

Finishing the main application does not automatically mean every other task is finished.

A short checklist can help:

- Confirm the application deadline and time zone.
- Check the school's instructions for transcripts, recommendations, and test scores.
- Record financial aid and scholarship deadlines separately.
- Ask the school counselor about the Early Decision agreement and school-office deadlines.
- Leave time to check the applicant portal for missing materials.

## Make room for the money conversation

Before committing, review the school's net price calculator and talk honestly about what your family can afford. Ask the financial aid office about circumstances the calculator may not capture.

[Johns Hopkins explains](https://apply.jhu.edu/how-to-apply/application-deadlines-requirements/early-decision/) that Regular Decision may be a better fit for students who want to compare admission and financial aid offers. Its ED plans require an agreement signed by the student, a parent or guardian, and the school counselor.

If an aid offer makes attendance unaffordable, contact the college about its review and release process. Read the actual agreement before applying so everyone understands the terms.

## Help your teenager choose with a clear head

A disappointing early result can create pressure to make the next decision quickly. I would give the student room to feel disappointed before asking them to make another commitment.

Then I would ask: What do you like about this school? Can you picture studying and living there? What questions remain?

ED2 can be a useful option when the answers point toward a clear first choice. Regular Decision can also give a student valuable time to consider options.

Our job as parents is to help keep the calendar manageable, ask thoughtful questions, and make space for an informed decision. The deadline tells us when to submit. The conversation helps us decide whether to submit.
