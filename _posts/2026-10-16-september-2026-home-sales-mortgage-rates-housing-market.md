*October 16, 2026 | Real Estate News & Housing Market Trends*

The U.S. housing market showed a small sign of improvement in September 2026, but rising mortgage rates continue to create challenges for buyers and sellers.

According to the National Association of REALTORS® (NAR), existing-home sales increased by **1.5% in September compared with August**, offering a modest boost after months of sluggish activity.

However, the improvement comes with a warning: Higher borrowing costs could slow housing demand and make homeownership less affordable.

In its October 13 article, [Home Sales Eke Out Uptick, But Mortgage Rate Threat Looms](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/economy/home-sales-eke-out-uptick-but-mortgage-rate-threat-looms), NAR examines the latest housing market trends and the challenges facing buyers and sellers.

Let's explore what the latest numbers mean for the real estate market as we head into the final months of 2026.

## September 2026 Housing Market: Key Highlights

The latest national housing statistics reveal a market that is gradually gaining inventory but continues to struggle with affordability.

| Housing Market Indicator | September 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Existing-home sales | 3.99 million (seasonally adjusted annual rate) |
| Monthly sales change | +1.5% |
| Median existing-home price | Up 1.3% year over year |
| Housing inventory | 1.60 million homes |
| Regional sales activity | Mixed across the U.S. |

*Source: [National Association of REALTORS®, September 2026 Existing-Home Sales Report](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/housing-statistics/existing-home-sales). The annualized sales rate describes the pace of sales, not the number of homes sold during September alone.*

These figures suggest that the housing market is not experiencing a dramatic recovery, but there are signs of changing conditions that could benefit some buyers.

## 1. Home Sales Increase, but the Recovery Remains Fragile

September's 1.5% increase in existing-home sales is encouraging, especially after a prolonged period of limited housing activity.

However, the improvement should be viewed in context.

NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun noted that August and September were the two slowest months for home sales so far in 2026, despite September's monthly increase.

This suggests that the housing market remains sensitive to economic conditions, particularly mortgage interest rates.

Higher financing costs can reduce purchasing power, preventing some potential buyers from qualifying for the homes they want.

**The takeaway:** A small increase in sales does not necessarily indicate a sustained housing market recovery.

## 2. Mortgage Rates Remain a Major Challenge for Buyers

Mortgage rates are among the most important factors influencing the housing market this fall.

When mortgage rates rise, monthly payments increase even if home prices remain unchanged.

For example, consider a hypothetical $400,000 home purchase with a 20% down payment and a $320,000 mortgage.

| 30-Year Fixed Mortgage Rate | Monthly Principal & Interest |
| --- | ---: |
| 6.0% | $1,919 |
| 6.5% | $2,023 |
| 7.0% | $2,129 |
| 7.5% | $2,237 |

*Illustrative calculations for a $320,000, 30-year fixed-rate loan, rounded to the nearest dollar. Payments exclude property taxes, insurance, HOA fees, and other expenses. These are examples, not current lender quotes.*

A difference of 1.5 percentage points could add approximately $318 to the monthly principal and interest payment.

For many households, that difference can significantly affect affordability.

Buyers who were previously approved for a particular price range may find themselves needing to reconsider their budgets as borrowing costs rise.

## 3. More Housing Inventory Creates Opportunities

One encouraging development is the growing number of homes available for sale.

NAR reported approximately 1.60 million homes in inventory in September 2026.

Increasing inventory gives buyers more properties to consider and may reduce some of the competition that characterized earlier housing market conditions.

Buyers may have greater opportunities to:

- Compare homes before making an offer.
- Request inspections and reasonable contingencies.
- Negotiate repairs or seller concessions.
- Evaluate price reductions on properties that have remained on the market longer.

However, increased inventory does not automatically mean home prices will decline.

Local demand, employment conditions, property type, and neighborhood characteristics continue to influence pricing.

## 4. Home Prices Continue to Show Resilience

Despite slower sales activity, the national median existing-home sales price increased 1.3% compared with September 2025.

This indicates that the national median sales price remains relatively stable, even as buyers face higher mortgage costs. It describes the homes that sold and does not establish how the value of every individual property changed.

For homeowners, the price data may be encouraging, but it does not guarantee future appreciation.

For first-time buyers, however, the combination of elevated home prices and mortgage rates can make entering the market especially challenging.

It's important to distinguish between a slowing market and a declining market.

Fewer sales do not necessarily translate into falling property values.

## 5. Regional Housing Markets Are Moving Differently

The national housing market is not experiencing the same conditions everywhere.

According to NAR, September existing-home sales increased in the South and West, remained unchanged in the Midwest, and declined in the Northeast compared with August.

Year over year, sales were lower in all four regions.

These differences demonstrate why local market knowledge matters.

A buyer in Maryland may encounter different conditions from someone purchasing in Florida, Texas, or California.

Even within Maryland, markets such as Howard County, Montgomery County, Baltimore County, and Anne Arundel County can experience different inventory levels, pricing trends, and buyer demand.

National statistics provide useful context, but neighborhood-level data should guide individual real estate decisions.

## 6. What Does This Mean for Homebuyers in Late 2026?

For prospective homebuyers, the current market presents both opportunities and challenges.

Higher mortgage rates have reduced affordability, but additional inventory and longer selling times may create more negotiating opportunities.

If you're considering purchasing a home, here are several strategies worth considering:

**Understand your buying power.** Get preapproved for a mortgage and evaluate monthly payments based on current rates rather than assuming rates will decline.

**Shop around for financing.** Compare mortgage lenders, loan programs, closing costs, and available rate-lock options.

**Look beyond the asking price.** Property taxes, homeowners insurance, maintenance expenses, and HOA fees can significantly affect your overall housing budget.

**Negotiate thoughtfully.** Depending on local conditions, some sellers may be willing to offer closing-cost assistance or address inspection-related concerns.

**Focus on long-term affordability.** A home purchase should fit your financial situation even if mortgage rates remain elevated longer than expected.

Waiting for lower mortgage rates is one possible strategy, but future rate movements are uncertain.

## 7. What Does This Mean for Home Sellers?

For sellers, September's housing data reinforces the importance of adjusting expectations to current market conditions.

With more homes available, buyers may be more selective.

Homes that are overpriced or poorly presented could face longer marketing periods.

Sellers should focus on three priorities:

**Price competitively.** Review recent comparable sales and active listings rather than relying solely on previous market highs.

**Prepare the property.** Clean, declutter, address necessary repairs, and use professional-quality listing photography.

**Remain flexible.** Consider reasonable negotiations, including closing-cost assistance or other concessions when appropriate.

A well-priced, well-maintained home can still attract serious buyers, even in a slower market.

## 8. Will Mortgage Rates Fall Before the End of 2026?

Mortgage rates remain one of the biggest uncertainties facing the housing market.

Their direction depends on several economic factors, including inflation, Treasury yields, Federal Reserve policy, and broader financial market conditions.

While lower mortgage rates could encourage more buyers to return to the market, there is no guarantee that borrowing costs will decline significantly in the near future.

Buyers and sellers should avoid making major financial decisions based entirely on predictions about interest rates.

Instead, focus on current affordability, personal financial readiness, and local housing conditions.

## Final Thoughts: A Changing Housing Market Requires a Thoughtful Strategy

September 2026 brought a modest improvement in U.S. existing-home sales, but the broader housing market continues to face challenges.

Rising mortgage rates are limiting affordability, while growing inventory is providing buyers with additional choices and potentially greater negotiating power.

For homebuyers, this may be an opportunity to explore available properties carefully and negotiate strategically.

For sellers, competitive pricing and effective marketing are increasingly important.

**The key takeaway:** The housing market is evolving, and successful real estate decisions require more than following national headlines.

Whether you're planning to buy, sell, or simply monitor property values, staying informed about mortgage rates, inventory, and local market trends can help you make confident, well-informed decisions.
